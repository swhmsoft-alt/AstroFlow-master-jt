## 2. Data-integrity problems found in the source study

Recorded so the same figures are not re-quoted downstream without review.

**2.1 Three incompatible "refit market size" figures in one document**
- USD 2.9bn/yr (Future Market Insights)
- USD 3–5bn/yr (Monaco Yacht Show / Informa)
- USD 5.13bn/yr (pmarketresearch / PW Consulting)

These are not refinements of one number — they are different market
definitions. Nothing was published.

**2.2 Arithmetically impossible revenue share**
The study states refit is "approximately 36% of superyacht industry revenue"
while also stating total industry output of EUR 54.0bn and refit of EUR 5.6bn.
36% × 54.0 = EUR 19.4bn ≠ EUR 5.6bn. The 36% figure was not published.

**2.3 Sources of unverifiable provenance**
`pmarketresearch`, `PW Consulting`, `Dataintelo`, and a "FutureFit" press
release could not be corroborated against a primary publisher. Where a figure
could not be traced to a primary source, it was excluded rather than softened.

**2.4 The defensible claim**
One driver survives scrutiny and is published: class societies require
in-water survey on a fixed cycle, and through-hull openings, seacocks and
shaft sealing areas are inspected every cycle. Replacement found at survey is
scheduled, not discretionary. That mechanism — not a market forecast — is what
produces repeat orders from a refit yard.

## 3. Prospect list (internal use only)

23 yards across the Americas, Mediterranean and Northern Europe were
identified in the source study. Retained here for sales routing.

**Priority reasoning from the study (not independently verified):**
- Highest-priority attack: yards that machine propulsion/shafting/rigging
  in-house, because titanium is bought as bar stock and cut on site.
- Fort Lauderdale and Palma are open-yard models where a third-party machining
  supplier is reachable at all; northern European yards run closed in-house
  service arms and are a poor first target.
- Gr2 → below-waterline fittings, seawater piping, heat-exchanger tube plates,
  wet exhaust. Gr5 → impellers, shafting, rig terminals, fasteners.

**Before any outreach:** verify each yard's current ownership, service
capability and contact independently. Do not rely on this list as-is.

## 4. Reusable lessons

1. A vertical landing page should sell *engineering judgement*, not market
   share. The grade-selection table earns more trust than any fleet count.
2. Refuse to publish numbers that cannot be traced. A missing figure costs
   nothing; a wrong figure costs the EEAT score across the whole domain.
3. "Yacht" and "Marine" are different verticals with different buyers. Keep
   `/industries/marine/` (subsea/offshore) separate from `/industries/yacht/`
   (surface-launched, class-society regime).
