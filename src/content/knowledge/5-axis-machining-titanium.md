## Why 5-axis matters for titanium

5-axis simultaneous machining becomes non-negotiable when machining titanium aerospace components with complex undercuts, thin walls, and contoured fillets. Compared to 3+2 indexed machining, 5-axis simultaneous:

- Reduces setups by 60-80% (one setup vs. three to five).
- Eliminates error stack-up between setups — critical for thin-wall blisk and IBR geometries where stack-up tolerance can exceed 0.05 mm.
- Allows shorter, more rigid tools, reducing deflection and tool chatter.

## Tooling & control requirements

For aerospace titanium 5-axis work, specify:

- **Thermal-stable spindle**: ≤ ±2 µm thermal growth from cold start to operating temperature.
- **Direct-drive or gear-driven spindles** (no belt slip under high torque transients).
- **A-axis and C-axis torque** sufficient to support the workpiece mass at full 0.001° indexing accuracy.
- **Coolant-through spindle at 70+ bar** for chip evacuation in deep-cavity operations.
- **Renishaw OMP60 or Heidenhain TS** touch probes for in-process datum verification.

## RFQ specification checklist

When requesting a quote for 5-axis titanium aerospace work, include:

1. Material grade + condition (e.g. "Ti-6Al-4V per AMS 4928, annealed").
2. Drawing with GD&T datums and critical-tolerance features called out.
3. Surface finish requirements per feature (Ra values).
4. NDT requirements (e.g. fluorescent penetrant per ASTM E1417, or FPI).
5. Heat treatment + aging specification.
6. Required certifications (AS9100D, DFARS, ITAR, customer-flow-down).
7. Annual volume and target unit cost.
